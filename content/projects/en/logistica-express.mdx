## The Business Problem

Inventory management in critical logistics operations often faces a fatal bottleneck: race conditions when updating stock in real time from multiple nodes. A traditional dashboard based on standard CRUD requests does not guarantee transactional integrity when dozens of operators decrement inventory simultaneously.

A system capable of handling absolute concurrency without locking the entire database was required, ensuring that warehouse operations at strategic points, such as San Javier or the Maule nodes, maintained perfect synchronization.

## Architectural Decisions

To solve this challenge, I designed an architecture focused on extreme performance and memory safety:

1. **Core Engine in Rust:** Languages with Garbage Collectors (like Node.js or Python) were discarded for the transactional core. Rust allowed the implementation of true atomic stock control through row-level granular locks and concurrency-safe data structures, guaranteeing predictable latencies.
2. **Reactive Frontend with Svelte 5:** The operators' dashboard needed to update screen information without reloading the full DOM or bloating the client. Svelte 5 allowed surgical reactivity and a tiny final bundle.
3. **Containerization with Docker:** The entire system was packaged into independent Docker containers, separating the database engine, the Rust backend, and the statically served client, facilitating frictionless deployment on any infrastructure.

## Trade-offs (Design Decisions)

| Approach | Advantages | Disadvantages / Costs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Atomic Control in Rust** | Zero risk of overselling. Extreme performance. | Slower initial development curve due to the *Borrow Checker*. |
| **Svelte 5 vs React** | Lower memory usage on the client; more natural reactivity. | Slightly smaller ecosystem of third-party libraries. |
| **Containerized Microservices** | High cohesion. Simple and replicable deployment. | Scaling is done per block, though for this volume it is optimal. |
